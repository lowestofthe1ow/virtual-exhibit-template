Most ALUs don't build a separate subtractor circuit. Instead they compute `A − B` as
`A + (¬B) + 1` — invert every bit of B, then add it to A with a carry-in of 1. That's the
definition of two's complement negation, and it means subtraction can share the exact same
adder hardware as addition.

## Why invert-and-add-one works

Flipping every bit of a 4-bit number and adding 1 produces its negative in two's complement.
So `¬B + 1` is just `−B`, and `A + (−B)` is `A − B`.

| B (binary) | ¬B (binary) | ¬B + 1 | Value  |
| ---------- | ----------- | ------ | ------ |
| 0011 (3)   | 1100        | 1101   | −3     |
| 0101 (5)   | 1010        | 1011   | −5     |

## Reading the flags

This exhibit reports **C** using the *no-borrow* convention: C is set when `A ≥ B`, meaning the
subtraction didn't need to borrow. Some real processors invert this convention — always check a
chip's datasheet before relying on its carry flag after a subtract.
